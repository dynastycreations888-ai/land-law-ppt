Introduction to Maharashtra Land Revenue Code 1966

The Maharashtra Land Revenue Code, 1966

Maharashtra Act XLI of 1966

Enforced: 1st May 1967

Scope & Application

  • Consolidates and amends law relating to land revenue in Maharashtra
  • Governs survey, settlement, assessment and collection of land revenue
  • Applies to all lands in Maharashtra except certain exempted areas
  • Replaces earlier Bombay Land Revenue Code provisions

Importance

  • Primary legislation governing land administration in Maharashtra
  • Establishes framework for revenue surveys and settlements
  • Defines powers and duties of revenue and survey officers
  • Provides for appeals, revision and review of orders
Structure: 15 Chapters, 336 Sections covering all aspects of land revenue administration

Survey Officers: Appointment & Hierarchy

Section 8: Survey Officers

"The State Government may appoint such officers designated as 'Settlement Commissioner', 'Director of Land Records', 'Deputy Directors of Land Records', 'Superintendents of Land Records', 'Settlement Officers', 'Survey Tahsildars', 'District Inspector of Land Records', etc."

Hierarchical Structure

Settlement Commissioner & Director of Land Records
Chief controlling authority for all settlement matters
Superintendent of Land Records
Supervises survey operations in assigned circles
District Inspector of Land Records
In charge of survey operations in district
Settlement Officer/Survey Tahsildar
Conducts actual survey and settlement operations
Subordinate Survey Staff
Assists in measurement, classification and record maintenance

Section 14: Powers and duties of survey officers, circle officers, etc.

"Survey officers shall exercise such powers and discharge such duties as are conferred and imposed on them by or under this Code"

Powers of Survey Officers

Section 80: General Notice and Summons Powers

"Survey officer may require by general notice or by summons, suitable service from holders of land, etc."

  • Issue general notices to landholders for survey cooperation
  • Summon any person to provide information about land
  • Require attendance during survey operations
  • Call for documents and records related to land holdings

Section 81: Assistance in Measurement and Classification

"Assistance to be given by holders and others in measurement or classification of land"

  • All landholders must assist in survey operations
  • Provide accurate information about land holdings
  • Allow access to survey officers for measurement
  • Help in identification of boundaries and landmarks

Section 241: Power to Enter Upon and Survey Land

  • Enter any land for survey purposes
  • Conduct measurements and mapping
  • Place boundary marks and survey stones
  • Take possession for survey operations when necessary

Additional Powers

  • Issue certificates and sanad for holdings
  • Conduct inquiries into land rights and titles
  • Determine boundaries in case of disputes
  • Maintain and update survey records

Duties of Survey Officers

Conduct Revenue Surveys

Measure, classify and assess all lands as per Section 79

  • Prepare detailed measurements
  • Classify land based on productivity
  • Assign survey numbers
  • Create village maps

Boundary Demarcation

Mark and maintain boundaries of survey numbers

  • Place permanent boundary stones
  • Maintain boundary marks
  • Resolve boundary disputes
  • Update maps with changes

Record Maintenance

Prepare and maintain all survey records

  • Village survey maps
  • Measurement books
  • Classification records
  • Rights records

Settlement Operations

Conduct settlement of land revenue assessment

  • Assess productivity of land
  • Determine revenue rates
  • Prepare settlement reports
  • Issue sanads to holders

Section 126: Village, Town and City Surveys

Specific Duties:

  • Survey lands within village/town/city sites
  • Determine titles and tenure of landholders
  • Identify encroachments on government land
  • Issue sanads confirming legitimate holdings

Compliance Duties

  • Follow prescribed procedures and timelines
  • Maintain confidentiality of survey operations
  • Submit regular reports to superior officers
  • Ensure accuracy and completeness of records

2

3

Field Operations

4

Inquiry Procedure

Determine rights, titles and interests

5

Settlement

Assessment and settlement of land revenue

Fix rates, issue sanads, prepare final records

Inquiry Officer Duties

  • Determine legitimate possession rights
  • Identify encroachments and unauthorized occupation
  • Decide on government vs private land classification
  • Issue appropriate orders and sanads

Settlement Principles

  • Based on productivity and soil classification
  • Consider local agricultural conditions
  • Follow prescribed rates and guidelines
  • Ensure equitable assessment across similar lands

Supreme Court Cases & Legal Precedents

Landmark Cases

State of Gujarat v. Patil Raghav Natha & Others (1989)

Supreme Court of India
Principle: Exercise of revisional powers under land revenue codes must be within reasonable time
Relevance: Applies to Section 257 powers of survey officers in Maharashtra

L.L. Constructions v. State of Maharashtra

Bombay High Court & Supreme Court
Principle: Survey officer's orders are subject to appeal and revision
Relevance: Establishes appellate hierarchy for survey decisions

Malhar Balkrushna Kulkarni v. Divisional Commissioner (2025)

Supreme Court of India
Principle: Section 257 powers must be exercised judiciously with proper notice
Relevance: Recent clarification on supervisory powers over survey officers

Key Legal Precedents

  • Survey operations must follow prescribed procedures strictly
  • Landholders have right to be heard during survey process
  • Survey officer's discretion is not absolute but guided by law
  • Appeals lie against survey officer decisions to higher authorities

Maharashtra-Specific Precedents

  • City survey operations in Mumbai and Pune have specialized procedures
  • Revenue Tribunal has jurisdiction over survey disputes
  • High Court exercises writ jurisdiction in appropriate cases

Modern Challenges & Compliance Issues

Solutions:

Solutions:
  • Specialized city survey procedures

  • Coordination with municipal authorities

Boundary Disputes

Increasing litigation over land boundaries

Solutions:
  • GPS-based mapping
  • Joint surveys with affected parties
  • Alternative dispute resolution

Time Constraints

Pressure to complete surveys within prescribed timelines

Solutions:
  • Adequate staffing
  • Streamlined procedures
  • Use of technology

Appeal and Revision Mechanisms

Sections 246-258 of Maharashtra Land Revenue Code
Appeal to Sub-Divisional Officer (Section 247)
Revision by Collector/Commissioner (Section 257)
Review powers of State Government (Section 258)
Writ jurisdiction of High Court

Compliance Requirements

  • Strict adherence to procedural requirements
  • Proper documentation and record maintenance
  • Timely disposal of applications and appeals
  • Regular training and capacity building
